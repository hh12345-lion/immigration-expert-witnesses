Historical country evidence can be relevant when assessing an individual's account of events that occurred in their country of origin. Political changes, conflict, state practices, social conditions and the treatment of particular groups may help explain the circumstances in which past events took place.

For immigration solicitors preparing an asylum appeal or related human-rights claim, an important consideration is how historical country information relates to the individual facts of the case. General reports may describe conditions during a particular period, but they do not necessarily establish what happened to a specific person.

A country expert may be able to provide specialist context where the relevant issues fall within their expertise. The role of that evidence is to explain relevant country conditions and, where appropriate, assess their relationship to the questions put to the expert. It is not to determine whether an individual's account is truthful or to decide the legal outcome of a claim.

This article examines how historical country evidence may assist in understanding past events, the considerations involved in assessing older sources, and the distinction between country expertise and the decision-maker's assessment of an individual case.

## What Is Historical Country Evidence?

Historical country evidence concerns conditions, events, practices or developments in a country during an earlier period. It may include material about political institutions, armed conflict, government policies, social structures, restrictions on particular groups or the conduct of state and non-state actors.

Sources may include:

- Reports published by recognised international organisations
- Historical research and academic publications
- Archived news reporting
- Official legislation, policy documents and public records
- Reports by human-rights organisations
- Research concerning particular regions or communities
- Material documenting changes in political or security conditions over time

The relevance of a source depends on the issue being examined, the period it covers, its reliability and the extent to which it addresses the question under consideration.

Historical material should not automatically be treated as evidence of present-day conditions. Equally, a source that is no longer current may remain relevant when the issue concerns events that occurred during the period it documents.

## How Historical Evidence Can Help Explain Past Experiences

Historical country evidence may be relevant in several ways, depending on the facts of the case and the questions requiring specialist consideration.

### Understanding the Political or Security Context

An individual's account may refer to events that occurred during a period of political instability, armed conflict, a change of government or a particular security operation.

Historical sources may help establish the wider context in which those events reportedly took place. For example, they may document the activities of particular authorities, restrictions imposed during a specific period or the treatment of people associated with a political movement.

This context does not establish that a particular individual experienced the events described. It may, however, assist in examining whether the account can be understood against the documented circumstances of the country at that time.

### Examining the Treatment of Particular Groups

Some asylum claims concern alleged treatment connected to political opinion, religion, ethnicity, nationality or membership of a particular social group.

Historical reports may document how a group was treated during a relevant period, including restrictions, discrimination, surveillance, detention or other forms of reported mistreatment.

The relevance of such material will depend on the individual's circumstances, the nature of the alleged events and the scope of the available evidence. Conditions affecting a group generally should not automatically be assumed to apply identically to every member of that group.

### Understanding Practices of State or Non-State Actors

Historical evidence may also describe the activities of state authorities or non-state actors during a particular period.

For instance, sources may document patterns of detention, the operation of security institutions, the activities of armed groups or the response of authorities to particular forms of opposition.

Where these matters fall within the expert's knowledge, a country expert may be able to explain the documented context and its possible relevance to the questions in the case. Any assessment must distinguish between documented patterns and conclusions about the conduct of a specific person or institution in an individual incident.

### Considering Social and Cultural Context

Past experiences may also be described in terms of social expectations, family structures, community relationships or practices affecting particular groups.

Historical research may help explain how these circumstances operated during the relevant period. This may be important where present-day sources do not adequately describe the context in which earlier events reportedly occurred.

Care is needed to avoid treating a country, community or cultural group as uniform. Practices and attitudes may vary by region, generation, social position and individual circumstances.

## Assessing the Reliability and Relevance of Historical Sources

The usefulness of historical country evidence depends not only on what a source says, but also on its reliability and relevance to the issue being examined.

### Consider the Date and Period Covered

The publication date of a source may differ from the date of the events it describes. A report published later may contain retrospective research, while an earlier source may provide contemporaneous observations.

It can therefore be useful to distinguish between:

- The date when the reported events occurred
- The date when the source was published
- The period covered by the research
- Any later information that confirms, qualifies or contradicts the original account

These distinctions can help clarify whether the material relates directly to the period in question.

### Examine the Source's Purpose and Scope

Sources are produced for different purposes. An official report, academic study, human-rights investigation and contemporaneous news report may each address different aspects of the same events.

Consideration of the author's expertise, the methodology used, the evidence cited and any stated limitations may help establish how much the source can reasonably contribute to the issue.

A source that documents general political conditions may not answer a more specific question about the operation of a local institution or the treatment of a particular individual.

### Consider Conflicting or Incomplete Information

Historical records may be incomplete, particularly where access to information was restricted, independent reporting was limited or events occurred in areas affected by conflict.

Sources may also differ in their accounts, terminology or assessment of particular developments.

Where material differences exist, it may be important to identify them rather than present one account as conclusively established without adequate explanation. The absence of a particular report or record does not, by itself, establish that an event did not occur.

An expert's assessment should explain relevant limitations and distinguish between what the available sources document and what remains uncertain.

## Linking Historical Country Evidence to the Individual Case

Historical information is most useful when its relevance to the questions in the case is clear.

A general account of political repression, for example, may provide background, but it may not address whether the particular person described in the evidence was known to the relevant authorities or affected by the documented practices.

Depending on the case, the questions requiring consideration might include:

- Whether the reported events occurred during a documented period of particular conditions
- Whether the individual belonged to a group affected by the developments described
- Whether the geographical location is relevant to the reported events
- Whether the sources address the type of conduct alleged
- Whether there is evidence of significant differences between the general pattern and the individual's circumstances
- Whether later developments alter the relevance of the historical material to the issues being considered

These are possible areas of inquiry, not a checklist that must be satisfied in every case.

The expert's role is to address the questions that fall within their expertise and the scope of their instructions. The decision-maker remains responsible for assessing the evidence as a whole and making the relevant findings.

## Historical Conditions and Present-Day Risk Are Different Questions

An important distinction is the difference between understanding past events and assessing conditions or risk at the time of a decision.

Historical evidence may be directly relevant to an allegation about what happened in the past. However, the fact that a particular practice existed during an earlier period does not necessarily establish that it continues today.

Equally, a change in government or official policy does not, without further examination, establish that all earlier risks have disappeared.

Where a case also concerns possible future harm, the relevant evidence may need to address subsequent developments, current country conditions and the individual's circumstances. The relationship between past experiences and present-day risk will depend on the facts and applicable legal framework.

An expert should make clear which period their evidence concerns and avoid presenting historical findings as proof of current conditions without an adequate basis.

## The Role of an Immigration Expert Witness

An immigration expert witness may be instructed where specialist knowledge of a country or a particular issue is relevant to a material question in the case.

Depending on the expert's qualifications and experience, the work may involve examining historical sources, explaining political or social developments, assessing regional differences or considering how documented country conditions relate to specific questions raised in the instructions.

The scope of the opinion should remain within the expert's expertise. The expert should identify the sources relied upon, explain the reasoning behind their conclusions and acknowledge material limitations where appropriate.

A country expert does not determine whether an individual's account is truthful or decide whether an asylum claim should succeed. Historical country evidence is one potential part of the material considered by the relevant decision-maker.

## Conclusion

Historical country evidence can help explain the political, social, security or institutional context in which past events reportedly occurred in an asylum case. Its relevance depends on the period covered, the reliability of the sources, the questions being examined and the relationship between the general country context and the individual's circumstances.

Careful analysis should distinguish between documented historical conditions, the interpretation of those conditions and findings about what happened to a particular person. It should also distinguish evidence about past events from evidence concerning present-day conditions.

Where specialist country knowledge may assist with a material issue, an immigration expert witness may be able to provide an independent assessment within the scope of their expertise. Whether such evidence is appropriate will depend on the circumstances of the individual case.
